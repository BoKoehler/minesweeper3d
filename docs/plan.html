Chondrite

Minesweeper as an excavation, rebuilt from the loop up so that three dimensions are the point rather than the gimmick.

A chondrite is a stony meteorite with mineral grains locked inside its matrix. You are digging them out. The rock is full of ordnance.

Diagnosis

Why every 3D minesweeper you have played is bad

They are all the same game: a cube of cells where a number counts mines in the surrounding 3×3×3 shell of 26. It reliably isn't fun, for four reasons that compound.

  1. OcclusionInterior cells have no pixels. You cannot click what you cannot see, so these games bolt on layer sliders and become file browsers.
  2. The constraint set is unrenderableThis is the real killer. In 2D a 2 refers to eight cells that are all on screen, in a ring you recognise instantly. In 3D a 7 refers to 26 cells, most buried behind other covered cells. Minesweeper deduction is visual set overlap — you watch two rings intersect and the answer falls out. Remove the ability to see the set and what is left is bookkeeping.
  3. Clearing everything is a choreExpert 2D is 480 cells. A 16³ volume is 4,096. Exhaustive clearing at that scale is work.
  4. There are no edges2D openings lean on walls and corners. A solid interior gives you nothing to push off.

Rendering tricks fix the first one. Nothing fixes the second except changing the rule.

The one change everything hangs on

Count six neighbours, not twenty-six

A revealed cell counts mines among its six face-adjacent neighbours only. Numbers run 0–6. The constraint set is a six-armed cross along the axes — a shape you can name in full without rotating the camera, even when four of the arms are buried in rock.

AdjacencyMaxShape of the constraint setReadable inside a solid?
2D, 8-way8a ring around the cellYes — the baseline
3D, 26-way26a shell of 26No — most cells are buried
3D, 18-way18a shell of 18No — same failure, one size down
3D, 6-way6a cross, one cell along each axisYes — all six are nameable

This is not a simplification — the pattern vocabulary changes

Under 6-connectivity the overlap structure between two readings collapses to three cases, and that is what makes it learnable:

Two revealed cells are…Shared cellsWhat it gives you
face-adjacent (distance 1)0but each sits inside the other's set — this is the cascade
two apart along an axis1the cell between them, pinned by subtraction
a diagonal step apart (√2)2the new 1-2-1: the workhorse pattern
a body diagonal apart (√3)0nothing — safe to ignore, which is itself useful

The entire vocabulary is 0 / 1 / 2. Teachable in one tutorial beat, useful for the whole game. A 26-neighbourhood has no such vocabulary — it has arithmetic.

And density gets a hard floor, for free

Flood fill spreads through zero-cells by face adjacency. Site percolation on the simple cubic lattice has a threshold near pc ≈ 0.3116. Let the fraction of zero-cells pass it and zero-regions connect — one click unzips the whole asteroid.

(1 − p)6 = 0.3116 → p ≈ 0.177

Mine density must stay above ~18% or cascades run away. Conveniently that is classic minesweeper territory — Expert is 20.6%. At p = 0.20 the numbers land at 0: 26% · 1: 39% · 2: 25% · 3: 8%, dominated by 1s and 2s exactly like the 2D game.

The threshold is for an infinite lattice and our hull is finite and irregular, so treat 18% as a floor to confirm by simulation, not as a proof.

The game

Five more rules, and the thing they add up to

Interaction

Only exposed cells are diggable

A cell can be dug if it touches open space — excavated void, or the outside. The diggable set is therefore always the visible skin of your own excavation. Occlusion is solved by construction, not by UI.

Objective

You extract cores, you do not clear the board

Some cells are cores. You win when every core is out. Nothing else needs digging, so the volume becomes a routing problem — pick an approach vector, tunnel toward a target — which is a genuinely three-dimensional skill the flat game cannot ask for.

Tension

Cores are visible from the start; the route is not

Cores render as marked positions glowing inside the rock. You always know where you are going and never know how to get there. That is the whole game in one sentence, and it reads in the first three seconds of looking at it.

Failure

A mine costs hull, not the run

Three hull points. A detonation destroys the cell and its six face-neighbours, chaining through any mines caught in the blast. Losing ten minutes to one misclick is minesweeper's worst property; three lives plus spectacular chain detonations turn it into risk management. A blast that takes a core loses that core for good — so recklessness is punished exactly where it should be.

Second information source

Sonar pings an axis

Spend a charge to ping an axis-aligned line through a cell; it reports the total mines on that line inside the hull. Line sums layer a global, nonogram-style constraint over the local crosses — and reading them means sighting down an axis, which is a camera move, not a menu. This is the move that could not exist in 2D.

The first ninety seconds, concretely

A rock hangs in space. Four brass markers glow somewhere inside it. You click the surface; a pocket of cells dissolves and leaves a small crater ringed with numbers. You snap the camera down the Z axis, spend a ping, and learn there are three mines on the line running straight at the nearest core.

You flag two you are sure of, dig the third wrong, and a chain detonation blows a cavity four cells deep. That is bad — hull is 2 of 3 — and also useful, because the new frontier sits much closer to the core than anything you would have dug by hand.

"My mistake opened a shortcut" is the feeling to build toward. It only exists because failure is destructive rather than terminal, and because the objective is a place rather than a completion percentage.

Charges regenerate at one per 40 cells cleared, so a careful player who opens big pockets earns more information than a player who tunnels blindly. Deep Core is the tier to design against — the others are its edges.

The quality bar

No forced guesses, ever

Minesweeper is only fun when deduction is always available. This is the highest-risk item in the project, and it gets built before a single triangle is drawn.

Generator loop. Seeded RNG places mines and cores → the solver attempts a full solve using only rules a human has → on a forced guess, perturb and retry → after N failures, drop density and restart.

Solver rules, in escalating cost order

  • Trivial. Count equals unknown neighbours → all mines. Count satisfied → all remaining safe.
  • Subset. For constraints A ⊆ B, the difference B\A holds n(B) − n(A) mines; resolve when that equals |B\A| or 0. Under 6-connectivity these come from exactly the axis-pair and diagonal-pair overlaps above.
  • Line sums. Sonar readings enter the same pool as ordinary linear constraints over 0/1 variables — no special case.
  • Global count. Total remaining mines constrains the whole frontier.
  • Bounded enumeration. Split the frontier into connected components; under ~22 unknowns, enumerate every consistent assignment. A cell that is a mine in 0% or 100% of them is forced. This is the expensive backstop and the reason generation runs in a Worker.

Three guarantees on top

  • The first dig is always a zero-cell, so the opening move opens a real pocket rather than a single square.
  • At generation time, at least one core is reachable by a deduction-only route.
  • Sonar is sufficient but not required — a tier must be solvable without spending every charge, so charges stay a strategic resource rather than a tax.
How it is put together

Architecture

Vite + TypeScript + a pinned Three.js, vanilla — no framework. The HUD is a DOM overlay, not WebGL text. Vitest on the core. Static build, no backend, GitHub Pages.

src/
  core/      pure TS, zero Three.js imports, fully unit-tested
    rng.ts        seeded PRNG — a seed reproduces a run exactly
    grid.ts       hull shape, indexing, 6-neighbour iteration
    generate.ts   mine + core placement, retry loop
    solver.ts     the five rules above
    reveal.ts     flood fill, frontier maintenance
    blast.ts      detonation and chain resolution
  game/      state machine over core: hull, charges, scoring, phases
  render/    scene, instances, digits, pick, fx
  ui/        HUD, menus, seed share strings, tutorial
  main.ts

Keeping core/ free of Three.js is the load-bearing decision: it is what lets the solver run in CI and generation run in a Worker.

Performance budget

  • Instancing. One InstancedMesh per visual class — covered, revealed, flagged, core. Four draw calls for the whole rock instead of four thousand.
  • Digits. A seven-glyph atlas (0–6) on instanced quads, one draw call, drawn only on exposed faces of revealed cells — a few hundred, not thousands. Billboarded and depth-faded.
  • Picking. Do not raycast the InstancedMesh; that is O(instances). March a DDA voxel ray through the grid instead — O(grid dimension), 20–40 steps, exact, allocation-free.
  • Generation runs in a Web Worker. The retry loop can take a while on Abyssal and must never block a frame.

Accessibility

A colourblind-safe digit palette plus distinct glyph weight, so colour is never the only channel. Flags and cores differ in silhouette, not just hue. Full keyboard play, with the cursor walking the exposed frontier cell by cell. prefers-reduced-motion disables camera shake and shortens dissolves.

Decisions that are yours, not mine

Three calls before I start

  1. 6-connectivity, or 18?

    Plan commits to 6 — 18 gives richer local constraints at the cost of the readable cross silhouette, which is the entire argument of this document. Worth a one-day spike at M6 if 6 turns out to feel thin.

  2. Real-time pressure, or pure puzzle?

    Currently out of scope — extracting a core could destabilise the rock and start a countdown, which is great for a climax and turns a puzzle into a pressure game. Prototype at M6 rather than commit now.

  3. Art direction

    Blocks nothing until M7 — clean instrument-and-survey, or chunky stylised voxel. It only sets the shader budget, so it can stay open while the game gets built.
